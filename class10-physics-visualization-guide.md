# Class 10 Physics (Bangladesh): Points, Formulas and Visualization Ideas

Chapter-by-chapter breakdown based on the NCTB syllabus. Written from knowledge of the curriculum, not from the book's page contents, so check each chapter against the actual book before building. Each chapter ends with an idea for what to visualize.

## 1. Physical Quantities and Measurement

- Fundamental vs derived quantities; SI base units (m, kg, s, A, K, mol, cd); prefixes (kilo, milli, micro, etc.)
- Density: ρ = m/V
- Vernier calipers: LC = 1 MSD ÷ number of VSD. Reading = MSR + (VSR × LC)
- Screw gauge: LC = pitch ÷ number of circular scale divisions. Reading = linear + (circular × LC)
- Zero error and its correction; significant figures
- Simple pendulum: T = 2π√(l/g)
- **Visualize:** a working vernier and screw gauge where the student drags the jaw and reads the value; a pendulum with adjustable length

## 2. Motion

- Distance vs displacement, speed vs velocity, acceleration
- Average speed = total distance ÷ total time; a = (v − u)/t
- Equations of motion: v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as
- Distance in the nth second: sₙ = u + a(2n − 1)/2
- Free fall: v = gt, h = ½gt², g ≈ 9.8 m/s²
- Projectile: T = 2u sinθ/g, H = u² sin²θ/2g, R = u² sin2θ/g
- Circular motion: v = ωr, ω = 2π/T, a_c = v²/r
- Relative velocity; distance-time and velocity-time graphs (slope = velocity or acceleration, area under v-t = displacement)
- **Visualize:** a live v-t graph linked to a moving object; a projectile launcher with angle and speed sliders; a circular motion vector animation

## 3. Force

- Newton's three laws; F = ma; 1 N = 1 kg·m/s²
- Momentum p = mv; impulse = FΔt = Δp
- Conservation of momentum: m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ (collisions, recoil, rockets)
- Friction: f = μN (static, kinetic, rolling); advantages and disadvantages
- Centripetal force: F = mv²/r
- Gravitation: F = Gm₁m₂/r², G = 6.67×10⁻¹¹; g = GM/R²; weight W = mg; g varies with height and depth
- Resultant of forces: R = √(P² + Q² + 2PQ cosθ); resolution of a force into components
- Torque = F × perpendicular distance; equilibrium; center of gravity; stability
- **Visualize:** a collision lab with elastic and inelastic modes; a force-vector parallelogram where the student drags the vectors; a friction block on an adjustable incline

## 4. Work, Power and Energy

- W = Fs cosθ (joule)
- KE = ½mv²; PE = mgh; work-energy theorem: W = ΔKE
- Conservation of energy; energy transformations
- Power: P = W/t (watt); 1 hp = 746 W; 1 kWh = 3.6×10⁶ J
- Efficiency = (useful output ÷ input) × 100%
- Simple machines (lever, pulley, inclined plane): MA = load/effort, VR = effort distance ÷ load distance, efficiency = MA/VR
- Mass-energy: E = mc²; renewable vs non-renewable energy
- **Visualize:** a roller coaster or pendulum with live KE/PE bars; a lever with draggable fulcrum

## 5. State of Matter and Pressure

- Properties of solids, liquids, gases; the particle model
- Elasticity: stress = F/A, strain = ΔL/L, Hooke's law (stress ∝ strain)
- Young's modulus Y = (F·L)/(A·ΔL); also bulk and shear modulus
- Pressure: P = F/A (pascal); liquid pressure P = ρgh
- Pascal's law and the hydraulic press: F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂
- Archimedes' principle: buoyant force = ρ_fluid × V_displaced × g; floating and sinking conditions
- Atmospheric pressure ≈ 1.013×10⁵ Pa; barometer, manometer
- Surface tension, viscosity, capillarity
- **Visualize:** a stress-strain curve you can stretch a wire along; a hydraulic press; an object floating in liquids of different density

## 6. Effect of Heat on Matter

- Temperature scales: C/5 = (F − 32)/9 = (K − 273)/5
- Heat Q = mcΔT; heat capacity = mc
- Latent heat: Q = mL (fusion, vaporization)
- Thermal expansion: ΔL = αLΔT; β = 2α (area); γ = 3α (volume)
- Anomalous expansion of water (max density at 4 °C)
- Gas laws: Boyle PV = const; Charles V/T = const; pressure law P/T = const; combined PV/T = const; PV = nRT
- Calorimetry: heat lost = heat gained
- Heat transfer: conduction, convection, radiation
- **Visualize:** a heating curve (temperature vs heat, with plateaus at phase changes); a gas in a piston with P-V-T sliders

## 7. Waves and Sound

- Wave types: mechanical vs electromagnetic, transverse vs longitudinal
- v = fλ; T = 1/f; amplitude, wavelength, phase
- Speed of sound ≈ 332 m/s at 0 °C, about +0.6 m/s per °C; fastest in solids, slowest in gases
- Echo: d = vt/2; the minimum distance for an echo is about 17 m
- Reflection, refraction, interference, superposition; resonance
- Pitch ↔ frequency, loudness ↔ amplitude/intensity (decibel), quality/timbre
- Audible range 20 Hz to 20 kHz; infrasound, ultrasound and their uses (sonar, medical imaging)
- **Visualize:** a wave generator with f, λ and amplitude sliders; two-wave interference; an echo timer

## 8. Reflection of Light

- Laws of reflection (i = r; incident ray, reflected ray and normal are coplanar)
- Plane mirror: image is virtual, upright, laterally inverted, same size; a rotating mirror turns the reflected ray by 2θ
- Spherical mirrors: terms (pole, focus, center of curvature); f = R/2
- Mirror formula: 1/f = 1/u + 1/v; magnification m = −v/u = h′/h
- Sign convention (check which one the book uses)
- Ray diagrams for each object position; uses (rear-view, shaving, headlights)
- **Visualize:** a draggable object with live ray tracing for concave and convex mirrors, with image properties shown

## 9. Refraction of Light

- Snell's law: n = sin i / sin r; n = c/v
- Real depth/apparent depth = n
- Critical angle: sin C = 1/n; total internal reflection (optical fibre, mirage, diamond sparkle)
- Prism: n = sin((A + δ)/2) / sin(A/2); dispersion, rainbow
- Lens formula: 1/f = 1/v − 1/u; m = v/u; power P = 1/f (dioptre)
- Combined lenses: P = P₁ + P₂
- Human eye; myopia (concave lens correction), hypermetropia (convex lens correction)
- Microscope and telescope
- **Visualize:** a ray bending at an interface with an n slider, including TIR; a lens ray simulator; a prism that splits white light

## 10. Static Electricity

- Charge: Q = ne, e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C; conservation and quantization of charge
- Charging by friction, conduction, induction; the electroscope
- Coulomb's law: F = kq₁q₂/r², k = 9×10⁹ N·m²/C²
- Electric field: E = F/q = kQ/r²; field lines
- Potential: V = W/q = kQ/r
- Capacitor: C = Q/V; parallel plate C = ε₀A/d; energy U = ½CV²
- Series: 1/C = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂; parallel: C = C₁ + C₂
- Lightning, lightning conductors, Van de Graaff generator
- **Visualize:** draggable charges with live field lines and force vectors; capacitor plates with adjustable d and A

## 11. Current Electricity

- I = Q/t; V = W/Q
- Ohm's law: V = IR; resistivity R = ρL/A
- Series: R = R₁ + R₂; parallel: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂
- Power: P = VI = I²R = V²/R; heat: H = I²Rt (Joule's law)
- Electrical energy in kWh and bill calculation
- EMF and internal resistance: ε = V + Ir, I = ε/(R + r)
- Kirchhoff's laws (if included); ammeter and voltmeter connections
- Fuse, circuit breaker, earthing, house wiring; AC vs DC
- **Visualize:** a drag-and-drop circuit builder with live current, voltage and power readouts; an I-V graph plot

## 12. Magnetic Effects of Current

- Magnetic field and field lines; Oersted's experiment; right-hand grip rule
- Field of a straight wire, a coil, a solenoid; electromagnets
- Force on a conductor: F = BIL sinθ (Fleming's left-hand rule); force on a moving charge: F = qvB
- DC motor
- Electromagnetic induction: EMF = −N(ΔΦ/Δt) (Faraday, Lenz's law), Fleming's right-hand rule
- AC generator and dynamo
- Transformer: Vₛ/Vₚ = Nₛ/Nₚ = Iₚ/Iₛ; step-up vs step-down; efficiency
- Power transmission: high voltage reduces I²R loss
- **Visualize:** a magnet moving through a coil with a galvanometer; a motor and generator animation; a transformer with turns-ratio sliders

## 13. Radioactivity and Electronics

- Atom and nucleus: Z, A, N = A − Z; isotopes
- α, β, γ: nature, penetration, ionizing power, deflection in fields
- Decay equations (balancing A and Z); half-life: N = N₀(½)^(t/T)
- Fission, fusion, mass defect: E = Δm·c²; uses (medicine, power, dating) and hazards
- Semiconductors (n-type, p-type), p-n junction, diode, rectification
- Transistor basics; LED, LDR
- Logic gates with truth tables: AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR, XOR
- X-rays and uses
- **Visualize:** a decay simulator (many atoms decaying randomly against the half-life curve); a logic gate sandbox; a diode I-V curve

## Tips for building it

- Give every simulation the same structure: **inputs (sliders) → formula engine → live outputs (graph + numbers)**. You write each formula once and reuse it.
- Show the formula with the student's actual numbers substituted in. This links the visual to the math they'll be tested on.
- The book's problem-solving sections follow standard patterns (given, to find, formula, substitution), so you could add a "check your answer" mode.
